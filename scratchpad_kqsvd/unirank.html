UniRank: Unified Rank Allocation for Low-Rank LLM Compression

Chao Han1, Haozhe Hu 1, Fei Ma2, Wei Zhang1, Xiaoyu Shen1

1Ningbo Institute of Digital Twin, Eastern Institute of Technology, Ningbo
2Guangdong Laboratory of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Economy (SZ)
Correspondence: Xiaoyu Shen xyshen@eitech.edu.cn Corresponding Author
Abstract

Low-rank decomposition serves as a promising compression paradigm for large language models, however, rank allocation remains challenging: manual rules lack generalizability, and learning-based approaches incur heavy computational overhead. To address these issues, we formulate global low-rank allocation as a sorting-and-truncation pipeline, and score each singular component via dual criteria: Local singular energy ratio that quantifies the intrinsic importance within the decomposed parameter matrix and Global functional importance (measured by input-output cosine similarity) that evaluates the functional significance of decomposed modules. We verify the strong correlation between high input-output cosine similarity and low effective rank through geometric interpretation and experimental validation. Furthermore, we propose rank-preserving fine-tuning, which performs direct LoRA tuning on decomposed weights and avoids extra information loss caused by re-truncation in conventional merging pipelines. Empirical results confirm that our method delivers sustained performance enhancements when combined with models featuring distinct decomposition schemes, model sizes and architectural designs, e.g. in one-shot compression without further fine-tuning, our method reduces perplexity by up to 50% compared with uniform and heuristic allocation baselines. Code will be available at https://github.com/EIT-NLP/LLM-Pruning.

UniRank: Unified Rank Allocation for Low-Rank LLM Compression

Chao Han1, Haozhe Hu 1, Fei Ma2, Wei Zhang1, Xiaoyu Shen1††thanks: Corresponding Author 1Ningbo Institute of Digital Twin, Eastern Institute of Technology, Ningbo 2Guangdong Laboratory of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Economy (SZ) Correspondence: Xiaoyu Shen xyshen@eitech.edu.cn

1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved state-of-the-art results across various domains, including question answering Lin et al. (2025b); Molfese et al. (2025), code generation Crupi et al. (2025); Zhang et al. (2025), and mathematical reasoning Ahn et al. (2024); Setlur et al. (2024). However, their high inference cost remains a major barrier to practical deployment Han et al. (2025). Low-rank approximation is an attractive structured compression technique because it replaces large linear transformations with compact factorized modules, preserves the original Transformer architecture, and can bring tangible inference acceleration Hu et al. (2026). Recent studies further reveal inherent low-rank structure in Transformer weights Smith et al. (2025); Saha et al. (2024), making low-rank compression a promising direction for efficient LLMs.

A practical low-rank pipeline must solve two coupled problems: how to decompose each weight matrix and how many singular components to keep for each matrix. While prior work has made substantial progress on decomposition design Jaderberg et al. (2014); Hsu et al. (2022); Zhang and Papyan (2025), the rank allocation problem remains less systematically explored. Existing strategies typically fall into three categories. Uniform allocation Wang et al. (2025) assigns the same rank ratio to all layers, overlooking the heterogeneous importance of different parameters. Heuristic allocation Li et al. (2024) relies on manually designed architectural or statistical rules, which may not generalize across models. Learning-based allocation Qinsi et al. (2025) relaxes discrete rank selection into continuous mask optimization, but it introduces substantial training overhead and is sensitive to initialization. These limitations call for a rank allocation method that is fine-grained, generalizable, and inexpensive.

Ideally, the optimal rank allocation can be obtained by evaluating model performance over all combinations of reserved singular vectors for all parameter matrices, yet such exhaustive combinatorial search is computationally intractable and infeasible for large-scale language models. Instead, we simplify the intractable global optimization problem into a concise Sorting-and-Truncation (S&T) pipeline over all singular vectors derived from full model weight matrices, on the premise of constructing a reliable scalar metric to quantify the importance of each singular component. Different from previous rank assignment strategies, our paradigm integrates both structural reconstruction and functional impact for comprehensive measurement.

Our core insight is that the overall importance of a singular vector depends on two critical factors. The first is local structural significance, which measures the singular vector’s contribution to reconstructing the original weight matrix. Following the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem Eckart and Young (1936), optimal low-rank reconstruction is driven by the magnitude of singular values, so we quantify this using the normalized squared singular value energy ratio, σk2/∑jσj2\sigma_{k}^{2}/\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}^{2}. The second is global functional importance, which assesses the host module’s impact on the overall network. To evaluate this without heavy computation, we exploit the intrinsic residual architecture of modern pre-norm LLMs Touvron et al. (2023a, b); Grattafiori et al. (2024); Yang et al. (2025); DeepSeek-AI (2026), formulated as Y=X+f​(X)Y=X+f(X) (Figure 1(a)). Due to the widespread use of scale-invariant normalization (e.g., RMSNorm Zhang and Sennrich (2019)), the semantic representation of information is strictly encoded in the directional vector space rather than its magnitude Ethayarajh (2019). Consequently, we use layer-wise input-output cosine similarity computed on a small calibration set as a lightweight proxy to quantify module redundancy. Unlike previous methods that use similarity for layer pruning Chen et al. (2025); Men et al. (2024); Zhao et al. (2025); Ding et al. (2026); He et al. (2026), we establish it as a reliable indicator of functional effective rank. As illustrated in Figure 1(b), a high input-output similarity implies that the layer transformation is either negligible (singular values ≈0\approx 0) or linearly correlated (effective rank ≈1\approx 1) with input features, both corresponds to a low effective rank.

Furthermore, existing low-rank compression methods suffer from a critical flaw when adapting to LoRA fine-tuning Hu et al. (2022). Since low-rank models are decomposed into two small factor matrices rather than full-rank weights, they fail to directly merge LoRA incremental parameters through standard weight reparameterization. Conventional schemes first reconstruct full-rank matrices by multiplying factors, integrate LoRA updates, then re-decompose and re-truncate to the target rank, which causes unavoidable information loss and extra computation Li et al. (2024). To address this, we propose Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning (RPFT), which splits the allocated rank kk into a tunable part kLoRAk_{\text{LoRA}} and a frozen part k−kLoRAk-k_{\text{LoRA}}. This design eliminates re-decomposition and perfectly preserves fine-tuned information without truncation loss, enabling efficient adaptation and no extra information loss.

We evaluate our approach by instantiating a full compression and adaptation pipeline, which we name UniRank. UniRank is designed as a modular framework: S&T provides a plug-and-play rank allocation strategy, while RPFT enables post-compression tuning without exceeding the pre-defined rank budget. Our contribution can be summarized as:

  • •

    We propose a global rank allocation framework that assigns low-rank budgets via a low-cost sorting-and-truncation pipeline.

  • •

    We provide both geometric interpretation and experimental valiadition that connects high input-output cosine similarity to low effective rank, offering a lightweight criterion for low-rank compression.

  • •

    We introduce Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning, a novel fine-tuning paradigm for low-rank models that enables efficient integration of LoRA updates on pre-decomposed factor matrices.

  • •

    Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. With merely 2 minutes of rank allocation overhead, S&T reduces perplexity by up to 50% in zero-shot compression compared to handcrafted baselines. Equipped with RPFT, the downstream performance is further boosted to 90% of the dense model at 25% sparsity, consistently outperforming other SOTA methods.

2 Related Works

Model compression mitigates LLM inference overhead, with sparsification split into unstructured and structured approaches. Unstructured sparsity Frantar and Alistarh (2023); Huang et al. (2025) trims individual weights, while structured sparsity Ashkboos et al. (2024); Men et al. (2024); Fan et al. (2026); Wu et al. (2026) prunes contiguous units for hardware efficiency. Low-rank compression decomposes original linear layers into two low-rank linear sub-modules, featuring inherent structural properties and favorable hardware compatibility.

Existing low-rank compression methods prioritize elaborate parameter decomposition. Recent findings Yu and Wu (2023); Jaiswal et al. (2025) demonstrate that activations, instead of model weights, carry intrinsic low-rank structures, motivating calibration-based activation decomposition as a mainstream research direction. Along this line of research, representative efforts explore diversified optimization paradigms to refine feature decomposition and model linearization, including covariance distribution alignment Ji et al. (2024); Wang et al. (2025), holistic structural decomposition Lin et al. (2025a); Saha et al. (2024); Smith et al. (2025) and outlier-aware pruning Zhang and Papyan (2025).

Rank allocation is another critical yet under-explored dimension in low-rank compression. Existing rank assignment strategies can be categorized into three mainstream paradigms: 1. Uniform allocation Yu and Wu (2023); Wang et al. (2025) uniformly reserves identical rank proportions for all parameters, widely serving as a standard baseline. 2. Heuristic methods conduct coarse-grained rank assignment across layers Lu et al. (2025); Mi et al. (2025); Tian et al. (2026) and parameter types Li et al. (2024) based on activation statistics. Though computationally efficient, they cannot accommodate fine singular-level properties. 3. Learning-based approaches Gao et al. (2024b); Qinsi et al. (2025); Li et al. (2026) support singular-level assessment yet incur substantial overhead. Visualization observations (as shown in Figure.4) further verify their initialization sensitivity and conservative assignment tendency. In this paper, we explore effective singular-level importance measurement, achieving efficient computation and better alignment of feature distributions across diverse modules and parameters

3 Method

In this section, we present the proposed UniRank framework. We detail the Sorting-and-Truncation (S&T) module for adaptive rank budget assignment. Then, we describe the Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning (RPFT) scheme designed to efficiently fine-tune compressed models without extra information loss. Figure 2 illustrates how UniRank works.

3.1 Decomposition

Given a weight matrix 𝐖∈ℝm×n\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n} from LLM linear modules (we omit layer and type indices for clarity), we perform singular value decomposition (SVD) to factorize it into low-rank components:

𝐖=𝐔​𝚺​𝐕⊤=∑i=1rσi​𝐮i​𝐯i⊤,\displaystyle\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}, (1)

where 𝐔∈ℝm×r\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r} and 𝐕∈ℝn×r\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r} are orthogonal matrices of left and right singular vectors, 𝚺=diag​(σ1,…,σr)\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\text{diag}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}) with singular values σ1≥σ2≥⋯≥σr>0\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{r}>0, and r=r​a​n​k​(W)≤min⁡(m,n)r=rank(W)\leq\min(m,n) is the full rank. A rank-kk approximation (k<rk<r) is 𝐖~k=∑i=1kσi​𝐮i​𝐯i⊤\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}, with approximation error proportional to the discarded singular values.

Sorting-and-Truncation (S&T).

We introduce S&T, a principled and architecture-agnostic strategy for adaptive rank allocation in low-rank decomposed LLMs. S&T operates by constructing a unified saliency metric across all parameters, enabling globally optimal rank assignment under budget constraint.

In Section 1, we have established that high input-output cosine similarity implies intrinsically low effective rank for a functional module, based on the geometric interpretation. To further validate this intuition empirically, we conduct a layer-wise correlation analysis: we measure the performance drop caused by rank truncation while keeping 90% singular energy per layer, and compare it with 1−𝔼​[cos⁡(x,y)]1-\mathbb{E}[\cos(x,y)] computed on the calibration set. As illustrated in Figure 3, the two metrics exhibit a strong linear correlation with a Pearson coefficient of 0.8792, corroborating that cosine similarity serves as a reliable indicator of layer sensitivity.

Accordingly, we define global functional importance as

ℐl=1−𝔼x∼𝒟c​[cos⁡(fl−1​(x),fl​(x))],\mathcal{I}_{l}=1-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{c}}\!\left[\cos(f_{l-1}(x),f_{l}(x))\right],

where fl​(x)f_{l}(x) denotes the output of layer ll, and cos⁡(⋅,⋅)\cos(\cdot,\cdot) denotes average cosine similarity over tokens. Intuitively, layers that induce larger feature transformations are assigned higher importance.

For local structural significance, we adopt the singular energy ratio: given decomposition results U​Σ​V⊤U\Sigma V^{\top}, each singular component (ui,σi,vi)(u_{i},\sigma_{i},v_{i}) contributes normalized energy σi2/‖Σ‖F2\sigma_{i}^{2}/\|\Sigma\|_{F}^{2}, reflecting how much variance it preserves.

S&T unifies these two factors into a aggregated importance score:

sl,i=ℐl⋅σl,i2‖Σl‖F2,s_{l,i}=\mathcal{I}_{l}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{l,i}^{2}}{\|\Sigma_{l}\|_{F}^{2}}, (2)

which quantifies the importance of the ii-th singular triplet in layer ll. All scores across the network are aggregated and sorted in descending order. Notably, this pipeline requires no gradient-based optimization or handcrafted architectural priors. Furthermore, S&T is fully decoupled from the base decomposition method and can be applied post-hoc to any SVD-based compressed model, making it a plug-and-play module for boosting rank efficiency.

Accumulative Rank Preservation

Following global sorting of all singular components, we observe that singular vectors from distinct weight matrices introduce heterogeneous parameter overhead. For an m×nm\times n weight matrix, retaining one singular vector pair consumes m+nm+n parameters. To address this variable parameter cost across matrices of different dimensions, we employ an accumulative rank preservation strategy. We first calculate the reserved parameter volume based on the total model parameters and target sparsity ratio, then iteratively select and aggregate the top singular vector pairs in descending order of importance until the total parameter consumption precisely reach the predefined budget.

3.2 Fine-Tuning

Low-rank compression decomposes weights into 𝐖~=𝐀𝐁⊤\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{\top}, with 𝐀∈ℝm×k\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k} and 𝐁∈ℝn×k\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}.

When LoRA meets Low-Rank Weights

In standard LoRA, a trainable low-rank incremental matrix Δ​𝐖=𝐀lora​𝐁lora⊤\Delta\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{A}_{\text{lora}}\mathbf{B}_{\text{lora}}^{\top} is added to a frozen pre-trained weight 𝐖\mathbf{W}, and during inference, Δ​𝐖\Delta\mathbf{W} is merged into 𝐖\mathbf{W} via re-parameterization, i.e 𝐖~=𝐖+Δ​𝐖\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{W}+\Delta\mathbf{W}. However, for low-rank compressed model, the original matrix is already decomposed into low-rank matrices 𝐀𝐁⊤\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{\top}, merging Δ​𝐖\Delta\mathbf{W} would require reconstructing a full matrix [𝐀𝐁]⊤+Δ​𝐖[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}]^{\top}+\Delta\mathbf{W}, and another SVD on the merged matrix, which would cause extra information loss (Appendix A.2 gives an detailed analysis on rank dynamics).

Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning (RPFT)

We present RPFT to fix this issue, which optimizes partial singular components under constant reserved rank. Each compressed weight satisfies 𝐖~=𝐀𝐁⊤=∑i=1kσi​𝐮i​𝐯i⊤\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}. we divide retained singular vectors into two sets: (1) ℐtrain\mathcal{I}_{\text{train}}: a small number (typically 8 or 16) of singular vectors with low importance scores. (2) ℐfix\mathcal{I}_{\text{fix}}: the remaining majority of singular vectors.

We directly optimize trainable vectors. The fine-tuned weight is written as:

𝐖~finetuned=∑i∈ℐfixσi​𝐮i​𝐯i⊤+∑j∈ℐtrainσj(t)​𝐮j(t)​(𝐯j(t))⊤,\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{finetuned}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\text{fix}}}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\text{train}}}\sigma_{j}^{(t)}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{(t)}(\mathbf{v}_{j}^{(t)})^{\top}, (3)

where superscript (t)(t) denotes trainable parameters. The rank stays bounded by kk to avoid rank growth and information loss. RPFT uses sparse trainable units and discards re-decomposition, effectively reducing computation cost.

4.2 Zero-Shot Direct Compression

We evaluate the zero-shot compression performance of our S&T rank allocation framework against traditional hand-crafted rank assignment strategies. All experiments are conducted without fine-tuning, exclusively reflecting the efficacy of rank allocation in preserving inherent model knowledge. As shown in Table 1, S&T achieves consistent and substantial perplexity reductions across all decomposition paradigms, with an average relative improvement of 52.9%. Even for weak baseline decomposition methods (e.g., naive weight SVD), S&T effectively alleviates catastrophic performance degradation, lowering the perplexity from 762.52 to 291.48 and demonstrating strong robustness to diverse factorization techniques.

4.3 Full Performance Evaluation

As shown in Table 2, our UniRank achieves competitive or superior performance against state-of-the-art baselines based on the Llama3.1-8b model. It obtains an average reasoning accuracy of 66.0%, surpassing LoRAP and ShortGPT by 3.0% and 4.0%, respectively. On WikiText, UniRank yields a perplexity of 10.22, ranking second among eight baseline methods and only slightly lower than SliceGPT (9.22%). Experimental results reveal that parameter-sparsity methods substantially outperform token-sparsity approaches, including MoD, D-LLM and SkipGPT, under moderate 25% compression, indicating that parameter redundancy dominates token redundancy at low sparsity. The performance gap between the two paradigms narrows at a higher 40% sparsity rate. Even so, UniRank remains competitive, achieving a reasoning accuracy of 59.13% and a perplexity of 14.03, which is comparable to the best baseline results.

Furthermore, UniRank consistently outperforms the LoRAP decomposition method at both 25% and 40% sparsity. At 25% sparsity, our method improves the average accuracy of seven reasoning tasks by 3% and reduces WikiText perplexity by 2.5. At 40% sparsity, it delivers a 2.6% accuracy gain and a 2.0 perplexity reduction. These consistent improvements validate the efficacy of the proposed UniRank framework.

4.4 Further Analysis

Generalization across decomposition.

We integrate UniRank with multiple decomposition methods on LLama2-7B. It consistently matches or surpasses each method’s original rank assignment, demonstrating strong generalization. This indicates our method effectively estimate both inter- and intra-parameter low-rank structures, yielding a near-optimal allocation. It is worth noting that heuristic manual assignment (reliant on model-specific observations) degrades on Llama3 (AWSVD on Table 2) versus Llama2 (AWSVD on Table 3) because of different attention architectures, our approach adapts automatically to different architectures, sustaining robust performance across models.

Rank allocation visualization

Our experimental analysis (Figure 4) on rank allocation visualization reveals distinct patterns across methods. The manual approach assigns uniform ranks per layer, heuristically allocating more to wv,wo matrices, such architecture-dependent heuristic limits generalization, explaining our method’s greater gains on Llama3.1-8B. The learning-based method exhibits a conservative strategy, with rank fluctuations despite global optimization. Given the significant layer-wise variability in low-rank properties, e.g., the singular value proportion for retaining 99% energy in wq, wk parameters ranges from 10% to 60%, such subtle adaptation is not enough to match the inherent low-rank structure. In contrast, our method shows a more aggressive and sensitive allocation, dynamically adapting to inter-layer and inter-parameter low-rank characteristics, which enhances compression efficacy.

Grouping Ablation for Global Singular Component Truncation

We conduct ablation on parameter grouping strategies for global rank allocation. We design three grouping settings: (1) parameter-level grouping, which sorts and truncates homogeneous projection parameters across all layers; (2) module-level grouping, which aggregates all parameters of the same functional module across layers; (3) our unified global setting, where all model parameters are ranked within a single group. Parameters across different groups are allocated rank budgets proportional to their original parameter sizes. Experimental results demonstrate that the unified global grouping consistently outperforms both parameter-level and module-level grouping. Separated grouping restricts cross-layer global singular component scheduling, leading to suboptimal rank allocation and inferior downstream performance. This validates the necessity of our holistic global rank assignment paradigm.

Hyperparameter Search for Global-Local Metric Balancing

While the base S&T metric, defined in Equation 2 exhibits strong generalization ability, we observe that minimal task-aware adaptation further enhances efficacy. We generalize S&T as:

sl,i=(ℐl)α⋅σl,i2‖Σl‖F2s_{l,i}=(\mathcal{I}_{l})^{\alpha}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{l,i}^{2}}{\|\Sigma_{l}\|_{F}^{2}} (4)

where adds α>0\alpha>0 for modulating the trade-off between structural importance and spectral energy concentration.

We perform an efficient grid search over α∈{0.0,0.2,0.4,⋯,2.0}\alpha\in\{0.0,0.2,0.4,\cdots,2.0\} using only a tiny validation subset (100 samples from WikiText trainset). As shown in Figure 5 (Llama2-7b with 50% sparsity), selecting α\alpha via validation loss can easily improves performance over the default α=1.0\alpha=1.0 configuration.

This refinement carries three key implications. First, it validates the compositional interpretability of S&T: both components encode meaningful signals whose optimal weighting is task-dependent. Second, it broadens UniRank’s applicability, i.e., when minimal validation data is available, practitioners gain a near-zero-overhead pathway to boost performance; when unavailable, α=1\alpha=1 remains a strong default. Third, it underscores UniRank’s efficiency advantage over learning-based rank allocation methods, which require expensive end-to-end optimization or gradient estimation. In contrast, a single scalar search suffices to adapt globally computed metrics to local task characteristics.

Ablation studies

We perform ablation experiments to validate the effectiveness of each UniRank component, adopting AWSVD decomposition with uniform rank allocation and standard LoRA fine-tuning as the strong baseline. We progressively integrate three core modules: local S&T relying only on singular energy ratio, full S&T incorporating global functional importance, and rank-preserving fine-tuning (RPFT). Results in Table 8 demonstrate consistent, additive gains at each integration step. S&T(local) outperforms uniform rank allocation by 1.61 average points, proving that singular energy-based rank assignment is more reasonable than uniform assignment. Full S&T further improves the score by 0.93 points over local S&T, verifying that high-importance layers with greater feature divergence require higher rank retention to preserve representation capability. Finally, substituting standard LoRA with RPFT delivers the most substantial gain (+3 points over baseline), as RPFT theoretically guarantees that the effective rank of each parameter weight remains strictly bounded by its allocated value.

Training cost and inference speedup

(1) Training Overhead. We evaluate training costs by comparing UniRank with LoRAP and DoBi on LLaMA2-7b under identical hardware platform (CPU: Intel Xeon w5-2445, GPU: NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada ×1\times 1). For LoRAP, UniRank adds only 2 minutes for rank allocation but saves 29 minutes in fine-tuning by avoiding secondary decomposition. For DoBi, which performs per-sample forward activation decomposition (nsample=256, seq_len=2048), training time scales with samples and epochs. Since DoBi requires 20 epochs for rank learning versus our single epoch, UniRank reduces this phase by 95% (19/20), demonstrating significant efficiency.

(2) Inference Acceleration. UniRank maintains the total rank count, so theoretical complexity matches baselines. Experimental results on various sequence lengths confirm this: compared to dense and sparse models (50% sparsity), speedup is consistent with minor fluctuations. Thus, our method achieves practical acceleration with performance gains.

5 Conclusion

This paper presents an efficient inference framework UniRank for LLM via low-rank decomposition. We propose Sorting-and-Truncation, which adaptively assigns ranks using a aggregated metric combining global functional importance and local singular energy, eliminating manual limitations and reducing computational overhead. Additionally, we introduce Rank-Preserving Fine-Tuning to enable parameter-efficient tuning without rank inflation or re-decomposition. UniRank demonstrates strong versatility by working seamlessly with various existing decomposition methods, whether applied to parameters or activations. Visualization reveals that the proposed method yields more aggressive rank distributions, validating its optimization efficacy. To sum up, UniRank offers a practical and generalizable solution for inference. Future work will extend its principles to even finer-grained compression (e.g. per-parameter importance evaluation) and broader architectural families.

Limitations

Despite promising practical performance, the proposed method is derived from geometric intuition rather than rigorous theoretical derivation such as error analysis. Moreover, the global functional importance is evaluated based on equivalent transformation of large modules instead of individual parameters. Such asymmetry constrains the performance ceiling. Specifically, trivial parameters may still exist inside critical modules, leaving further pruning potential untapped. We will explore this issue in future work and wish to provide insights for relevant research communities.

Appendix A Appendix

A.1 Baseline Methods

To comprehensively validate the effectiveness of our method, we compare it with representative state-of-the-art techniques covering static model compression and dynamic computation allocation paradigms, which are detailed as follows:

Low Rank Compression

  • •

    AFM Yu and Wu (2023): it reveals that Transformer activations, rather than weights, possess intrinsic low-rank structure. And it proposes Atomic Feature Mimicking to aggregate activation statistics for effective low-rank decomposition.

  • •

    LoRAP (Kim et al., 2024): it adopts differentiated structured compression for Transformers, applying low-rank decomposition to attention modules and channel pruning to MLP modules.

  • •

    Dobi Men et al. (2024): it establishes a fully activation-centric compression paradigm, performing differentiable SVD and truncation directly on activations instead of weights. It is worth noting that the DoBi method requires performing SVD decomposition on every linear layer’s forward activation using a calibration set. Such heavy computation cannot be supported on our experimental platform. Accordingly, we only apply DoBi to attention modules and exclude MLP modules from compression. This setting is adopted for both the original DoBi and DoBi+unirank to guarantee fair comparisons.

Static Pruning

  • •

    SliceGPT Ashkboos et al. (2024): it eliminates entire parameter rows and columns by applying PCA to orthogonally transformed model parameters.

  • •

    Shortened-llama (Kim et al., 2024): it constructs lightweight models by pruning consecutive network layers.

  • •

    ShortGPT Men et al. (2024): it adopts Block Influence (BI) to quantitatively assess layer importance and eliminate insignificant layers.

Dynamic Computation Allocation

  • •

    Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) Raposo et al. (2024): it utilizes a greedy routing strategy and maintains a fixed sparsity ratio for each layer block to dynamically determine the execution or skipping of computational units for individual tokens.

  • •

    D-LLM (Jiang et al., 2024): it realizes global adaptive sparsity by dynamically adjusting layer-wise computation based on input features

  • •

    SkipGPT Zhao et al. (2025): it achieves finer-grained dynamic control by decoupling intra-layer attention and MLP modules.

A.3 Attention-MLP Decoupling

We further decouple each Transformer layer into individual attention and MLP modules, both adhering to the residual structure for similarity-based rank allocation. Statistically, the attention module yields a higher average input-output cosine similarity (94%) than the MLP module (89%), indicating a lower effective intrinsic rank and better low-rank compressibility. This aligns with LoRAP observations and validates our similarity-driven rank assignment paradigm. The marginal overall improvement (i.e. perplexity reduce 0.4) stems from slight parameter budget reallocation toward MLP after decoupling. Quantitatively, the divergent singular components between layer-wise and module-wise allocation only account for 2.1% of the total, leading to negligible performance discrepancy.
